---
title: Battery saver
description: Two levels of savings, switched by hand or when the battery runs low.
---

Battery saver trades the map's extras for battery life. Turn it on in **Settings → Behavior → Battery saver**, from the command palette ("Battery saver"), or let it turn itself on when the battery gets low. While it's on, a leaf button sits next to the weather on the map; tap it to get back to the setting.

Your own settings are never changed. Battery saver holds things off while it's on, and everything returns as you left it when it turns off.

---

## Levels

| | On | Extreme |
|---|---|---|
| 3D terrain, buildings, trees and shadows | Off | Off |
| Map sharpness | 1.5× pixel density | 1× pixel density |
| Map frame rate | Up to 30 fps | Up to 30 fps |
| Your location | Wi-Fi and cell, no GPS | Wi-Fi and cell, no GPS, refreshed less often |
| Live vehicles on a route or trip | Every 15 s | Every 30 s |
| Basemap | Usual style | Grey roads, paths and place names on black |
| App theme | Your choice | Dark |
| Animations | On | Off |

Navigation always gets full GPS accuracy, whatever the level, so turn-by-turn stays precise. Its own frame-rate and 3D limits follow the level too: On caps the frame rate, Extreme also drops the tilted 3D camera.

## Turning on automatically

**Turn on when battery is low** switches battery saver on below one level (20% by default) and Extreme below another (10%), and only while the device isn't charging. A short message says why when it happens. A level you set by hand always wins if it's stronger.

The automatic option needs the browser to report the battery level. Chrome and Android do; Safari and iOS don't, so on an iPhone the option is hidden and battery saver is switched by hand.

## Measured savings

Measured in headless Chrome on a shared dev machine, against battery saver off. Processor time covers the page, GPU and browser processes; GPU calls count every WebGL call the map makes, which tracks the work sent to the graphics chip.

| Scenario | On | Extreme |
|---|---|---|
| Opening the map | 44% less processor time, 82% fewer GPU calls | 75% less, 99% fewer |
| Sitting idle on a flat map | 24% less processor time | 91% less |
| Tilting into a 3D city | 51% less, 71% fewer GPU calls | 96% less, 99% fewer |
| Panning around | 36% less, 62% fewer GPU calls | 88% less, 91% fewer |

Tile downloads while panning fell from 22 MB to 3.7 MB (On) and 2.3 MB (Extreme). Screen power, which Extreme's black map saves on OLED displays, and GPS power need a real device to measure.
