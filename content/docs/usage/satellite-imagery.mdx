---
title: Satellite imagery
description: Choose which aerial and satellite sources the satellite map draws, add your own, and set fallbacks for areas they don't cover.
---

The satellite and hybrid maps draw from a **stack** of imagery sources, set under
**Settings → Appearance → Style → Satellite imagery**. The top source wins. Where
it has no imagery, the next one down fills in, and so on, so a sharp regional
source can sit on top of a coarser global one. The stack syncs with your account.

## Built-in sources

| Source | Coverage | Detail | License |
|---|---|---|---|
| **USGS NAIP** | Contiguous US, zoom 13 and closer | 0.3–0.6 m | Public domain |
| **Sentinel-2 cloudless** (EOX, 2016) | Worldwide | 10 m | CC BY 4.0 |
| **USGS Imagery** | US, up to zoom 16 | ~1 m | Public domain |
| **Esri World Imagery** | Worldwide | Up to ~0.3 m in cities | Needs the [Esri integration](/docs/usage/integrations/esri) |
| **Google Photorealistic 3D** | Worldwide, 3D in most cities | 3D meshes | Premium; needs the [Google Maps integration](/docs/usage/integrations/google-maps) |

The default stack is USGS NAIP over Esri World Imagery: public-domain aerial
photos across the contiguous US, and Esri everywhere else. Esri appears only
once the Esri integration has a key; without one, the satellite map shows NAIP
alone. USGS Imagery fills areas outside its coverage with white, so put it
at the bottom of a stack, or use it alone.

## Google Photorealistic 3D

Google's 3D tiles are a Premium source: only users with the
`premium:photorealistic_3d` permission (the Premium role) see them in the
imagery settings, and the proxy answers anyone else with `401` or `403`.

Google's 3D tiles are meshes rather than images, so they are drawn by their own
layer instead of taking a place among the raster sources. From zoom 12 in,
only the mesh is drawn, with no flat imagery under it. Below zoom 12, where
the map is a globe and the 3D tiles are not drawn, the rasters show instead. While the mesh is on
screen, Parchment's own 3D buildings, trees, landmarks and bridge decks are
hidden, since the mesh already contains them, and the Google Maps logo and the
data providers' credits appear beside the map's info button.

In hybrid with 3D terrain on, street names and one-way arrows on a bridge are
raised onto Google's bridge, using the same bridge deck data that lifts them on
the standard map. The decks themselves stay hidden.

The tiles go through the server's `/proxy/google/3dtiles/` route, which checks
the user's session and adds the Google Maps integration's key, so the key needs the **Map Tiles API** enabled.
Google bills each session (one root tileset request, good for about three
hours): 1,000 free a month, then $6 per 1,000. The tiles honour Google's own
caching headers and are never saved for offline use.

The map's camera does not know about the mesh, so at a steep pitch among tall
buildings it can end up inside one.

## Adding your own

Create a raster layer in **Library → Layers → New layer**, or choose **Add →
New source…** in the imagery settings. Then, under **Options**, turn on
**Satellite imagery**. An imagery layer is never drawn as an overlay, and a new
one goes to the top of your stack.

Tile URLs can use `{z}/{x}/{y}` or, for WMS and ArcGIS export endpoints,
`{bbox-epsg-3857}`:

```text
https://example.com/ImageServer/exportImage?bbox={bbox-epsg-3857}&bboxSR=3857&imageSR=3857&size=512,512&format=png32&f=image
```

For a source to work as a fallback, it should return no tile, or a transparent
one, where it has no imagery. Set `bounds`, `minzoom` and `maxzoom` on the
source to limit where it is requested.

Satellite imagery is drawn by the MapLibre engine only. Mapbox uses its own
satellite style.
