---
title: Satellite imagery
description: Choose which aerial and satellite sources the satellite map draws, add your own, and set fallbacks for areas they don't cover.
---

The satellite and hybrid maps draw from a **stack** of imagery sources, set under
**Settings → Appearance → Style → Satellite imagery**. The top source wins. Where
it has no imagery, the next one down fills in, and so on, so a sharp regional
source can sit on top of a coarser global one. The stack syncs with your account.

## Built-in sources

| Source | Coverage | Detail | License |
|---|---|---|---|
| **USGS NAIP** | Contiguous US, zoom 13 and closer | 0.3–0.6 m | Public domain |
| **Sentinel-2 cloudless** (EOX, 2016) | Worldwide | 10 m | CC BY 4.0 |
| **USGS Imagery** | US, up to zoom 16 | ~1 m | Public domain |
| **Esri World Imagery** | Worldwide | Up to ~0.3 m in cities | Esri terms of use |
| **Esri Clarity** | Worldwide | Often newer than World Imagery | Esri terms of use |

The default stack is USGS NAIP over Esri World Imagery: public-domain aerial
photos across the contiguous US, and Esri everywhere else. USGS Imagery fills areas outside its coverage with white, so put it
at the bottom of a stack, or use it alone.

## Adding your own

Create a raster layer in **Library → Layers → New layer**, or choose **Add →
New source…** in the imagery settings. Then, under **Options**, turn on
**Satellite imagery**. An imagery layer is never drawn as an overlay, and a new
one goes to the top of your stack.

Tile URLs can use `{z}/{x}/{y}` or, for WMS and ArcGIS export endpoints,
`{bbox-epsg-3857}`:

```text
https://example.com/ImageServer/exportImage?bbox={bbox-epsg-3857}&bboxSR=3857&imageSR=3857&size=512,512&format=png32&f=image
```

For a source to work as a fallback, it should return no tile, or a transparent
one, where it has no imagery. Set `bounds`, `minzoom` and `maxzoom` on the
source to limit where it is requested.

Satellite imagery is drawn by the MapLibre engine only. Mapbox uses its own
satellite style.
