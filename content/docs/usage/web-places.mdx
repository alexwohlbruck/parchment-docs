---
title: Places from the web
description: When Ask can't find a business on the map, it looks for the business's own website and offers to add it to OpenStreetMap.
---

Ask searches the map first. When a business you name isn't there, it calls
`find_place_on_web`. You can also start it from search: under text results
(or on their own when there are none), **Find “…” on the web** opens a new Ask
chat headed **Find on the web** and the name you searched. The prompt behind it
is written on the server; the app sends only the name and the `find-on-web`
skill. When the lookup finds the place, its page opens on its own, and Back
returns to the chat.

Each stage shows as a step in the chat while it runs: **Finding the website**,
**Reading the website**, **Figuring out what kind of place it is**, **Getting
the details** (with the kind of place and the details the site gave, like
"Restaurant · cuisine, reservations"), **Finding the address** and **Checking
the map**. The lookup:

1. Searches the web for the business's **own website**. Search results are only
   used to find that site. Directory, review, booking, delivery and social
   sites (Google, Yelp, Foursquare, Tripadvisor and the like) are never used
   as a source.

   A business with no website of its own often has only a Facebook page or an
   Instagram profile, and those count as its own site: what the business says
   of itself. Only the page itself is read, from what it serves without
   signing in (the title and description, where a Facebook page or an
   Instagram bio usually gives the street address). No other pages there are
   followed, and the link goes into `contact:facebook` or `contact:instagram`
   rather than `website`.

   The search returns the website and the business's own social pages, and
   each is tried in turn until one gives a street address. A Facebook page
   with only a city falls through to the Instagram bio, and a namesake's
   website in another state falls through to the right business's pages.
2. Reads the site. Its schema.org JSON-LD comes first; the homepage and up to
   two pages on the same site are checked, locations, visit, hours and contact
   pages ahead of an about page.

   When a site lists several locations, the model is told what was searched
   ("La Cabra, Chelsea") and where, and reads only the location its
   neighbourhood or city names, else the nearest one. That location's own name
   goes into OSM's `branch` tag (`branch=Chelsea`), and only if the page
   states it. Among several locations in the site's JSON-LD, one in the
   named area comes first.

   When those pages don't give the named location's street ("Joe Coffee,
   Upper West Side" finding only the Village cafe), the model opens the site's
   locations page if it hasn't yet, picks that location's own page from the
   site's links, subdomains included, and reads it. If the site has
   no structured data, the model reads the page text (see
   [Checking what the model reads](#checking-what-the-model-reads)).
   Pages that ship as a script-unpacked bundle (their markup as a JSON string
   and their images inline) are unpacked first, so their text and images can
   be read like any other page.

   If the text gives no street address, up to two of the site's images are
   read by the model: ones whose alt text, file name or class mentions a logo,
   sign, location, map, hours or contact details, ahead of those in the header
   or footer. Icons, social badges and photos are skipped. Each image is shrunk
   to 1000 px across. Models that can't read images skip this step.

   An address you give Ask yourself ("it's at 215 Union St, Brooklyn") stands
   in for one the site doesn't state. Only an address you typed counts: Ask
   can't pass one it found on another website.
3. Checks the result: the name must match, allowing one slipped or swapped
   letter in a word of five or more ("bookie lab" finds Boogie Lab) and a
   kind of place typed after the name ("Ruby's cafe" finds Ruby's), the address must be within 60 km of
   where you're looking, and the site must not say the business has closed.
   The address only counts when a geocoder finds that house number on that
   street, so 119 Avenue A never lands on 119 New York Avenue. Each geocoder
   is tried in turn until one does, searching near where you're looking. A
   floor and door after the house number ("Århusgade 48 st. th") are left
   out of the search. The address is then spelt the way the geocoder spells
   it ("215 UNION ST" becomes "215 Union Street"), keeping the city the site
   gives.
   The place is named the way the site names itself, not the way you typed
   it. Its JSON-LD name comes first, then its declared site name, logo and
   page title, taking the fullest form when the added words are more name
   ("Boogie Lab Bakery & Bistro") rather than a description ("coffee cup").
   A logo linking home ("Fairfax Home") and titles that describe the place
   ("Bakery in New York, NY") don't count, and a name in capitals takes the
   site's other spelling or title case ("SMOR Bakery" becomes "Smør Bakery").
   It drops a country or company form the site appends ("La Cabra USA" and
   "Joe's Pizza LLC" become "La Cabra" and "Joe's Pizza"), and is placed at the geocoded point for its
   house number, falling back to the coordinates the site gives.
4. Looks for the same name in OpenStreetMap within 300 m. If it's there, Ask
   cites that place instead.

What's left is a place that isn't on the map yet. Ask cites it like any other,
and its page says **Not on the map yet**, with two actions:

- **Add to map** opens the add-a-place form with the type, name, address,
  phone, email, opening hours and website already filled in. You check them and
  submit under your own OpenStreetMap account.
- A site that says it hasn't opened yet ("coming soon", "opening this fall")
  shows **Opening soon** on the card, and the business goes to OpenStreetMap
  under the `construction:` lifecycle prefix, e.g. `construction:amenity=cafe`.
- **Update map** takes its place when OpenStreetMap already has a node or way
  at the same house number and street within 60 m with no business on it now:
  a plain address point, a `shop=vacant`, or a closed business under
  `disused:*` or `old_name`. Named buildings and entrances are never taken
  over. The edit form opens on that element with the former tenant's tags
  removed (lifecycle prefixes, `old_name`, name, contact, hours, cuisine,
  brand and operator), the mapped address kept as it is, and the business's
  tags added. If the element has been deleted since, the add-a-place form opens
  instead.
- **Tell mappers** opens a new OpenStreetMap note at the place for someone
  else to map it. The note says what's missing, whether it hasn't opened yet,
  and which element it takes over, then lists every suggested tag as
  `key=value`, one per line: the type and name first, the address last, so a
  mapper can paste them into an editor.

Nothing is sent to OpenStreetMap until you submit one of these yourself.

## Checking what the model reads

The model never decides what a tag means on its own. Everything it returns is
checked against OpenStreetMap's [iD tagging schema](https://github.com/openstreetmap/id-tagging-schema)
and the page text before it reaches the edit:

- **Type.** The model picks from the schema's own list of business presets.
  Anything not on that list is dropped, and the mapper picks the type.
- **Name, address and contact details.** Each has to appear in the page text.
  Phone numbers are compared digit by digit.
- **Opening hours.** These need a quote from the page and must be valid
  `opening_hours` syntax that the parser reads without correcting anything.
  Ranges that overlap within a day are rejected. Days with the same hours are
  merged (`Mo-Su 08:00-17:00`), a day with a second sitting keeps both ranges
  (`We-Sa 08:30-15:30,17:30-22:00`), and a JSON-LD day that opens and closes
  at midnight is read as closed.
- **Other details.** The model is given every field of the chosen preset, with
  the values the schema allows, and has to answer each one. The answer is
  either null or a value with the exact words from the page that state it.
  Fields it skips are asked again once. An answer is kept only if:
  - the field belongs to the preset;
  - the value is one the schema allows, or is named in the quote;
  - the quote is really on the page.

Fields a website can't settle, such as `level`, `building` and reference
numbers, are never asked about. Neither are `brand` and `branch`, which belong
to chains.

## Why only the business's own site

OpenStreetMap can't take data copied from Google, Yelp, Foursquare and other
listings, because of their licences. Facts a business publishes about itself
on its own site are what a mapper would use, so only those fill in the edit.

## How long a place lasts

A place found this way isn't stored. The server keeps it in memory for a day so
its page and links work; after that, or after a restart, Ask finds it again.
Its id is `web/<hash>`, and `GET /places/details?source=web&id=<hash>` returns
it while it's kept.

## Over MCP

MCP clients get `find_place_on_web` too. Parchment has no model to search the
web with there, so pass the business's `website` yourself.
