---
title: Offline maps
description: Save an area of the map, then see it, search it and get directions in it with no connection.
---

Open **Offline maps** from the sidebar, or from the dashboard on a phone, then
choose **Save an area**.

1. Move and zoom the map until the area you want fills the square. The square
   stays still while the map moves, so zooming out saves a bigger area.
2. Pick what to include. The map itself is always saved. Layers that are on
   the map now start switched on: transit lines, cycling, street imagery,
   3D terrain and any custom layer with map tiles.
3. Under **Use offline**, keep **Search places** and **Get directions** on to
   search the area and plan trips in it with no connection. Turn them off to
   save only the map.
4. Under **Keep**, choose how long to keep it: until you delete it, for a
   week, a month, 3 months or a year, or until a date you pick. It is deleted
   at the end of that day.
5. Check the size estimate under the switches, then tap **Download**. The bar
   above the button shows how much each layer and feature adds.

The area is named after the place in its middle; rename it from its menu. A
square that would take too long to save turns red, and you need to zoom in
before you can download it.

## Managing saved areas

Saved areas are drawn on the map while **Offline maps** is open. Tap one, on
the map or in the list, to edit it: move the square, switch layers or features
on and off, or change how long it's kept. **Save changes** downloads only what
changed, and drops what the area no longer needs.

Each saved area shows its size, and either when it was last updated or, when
it holds transit times, the last day they cover. From its menu you can:

- **Update** it to pick up the latest map data
- **Rename** or **Delete** it
- Change how long it's kept

A download keeps going after a reload. If the connection drops it waits, and it
carries on once you're back online. Tap pause to stop it yourself.

Overlapping areas share what they have in common, so saving a neighbourhood
inside a city you already saved costs very little.

## What works offline

- The map and the layers you saved, inside the saved area
- Your location on the map
- **Search** for places, streets and transit stops in saved areas. Your saved
  and recent places come first.
- **Place pages** with the address, opening hours, phone and website
- **Departures** at a stop or station, from the timetable
- **Directions** on foot, by bike and by transit, when both ends are inside
  one saved area

Offline departures and transit trips use scheduled times, not live ones. A
saved area holds two weeks of timetable, and Parchment fetches a fresh one
every few days whenever you're online, so it doesn't run out. Driving
directions, reviews, photos and live arrivals still need a connection. Place
pages, departures and directions answered from a saved area say so, since they
can be missing details, live times and closures.

## Keeping saved maps

Parchment asks the browser to keep saved maps when space runs low. On iPhone
and iPad, add Parchment to your home screen: Safari clears data for sites you
haven't opened in a while, but not for apps on the home screen.

Some layers can't be saved, because their providers don't allow it. These are
the OpenStreetMap standard tiles, Esri satellite imagery, OpenTopoMap,
OpenRailwayMap, OpenSeaMap and Mapbox layers. They are left out of the list.

## How it works

Saving an area lists every tile each chosen layer has inside the square, from
zoom 0 to the layer's deepest zoom (up to 16; 12 for terrain). It then saves the
tiles, plus the fonts, icons and manifests the map needs, to a Cache Storage
bucket called `offline-regions`. The service worker answers from that bucket
before anything else, so a saved tile is drawn with or without a connection.
Online, it refreshes the saved copy in the background.

The size estimate measures one real tile per layer at the centre of the square
and scales it for each zoom level.

Search and directions come from three packages the server builds for the
square and saves next to the tiles:

- **Places**: every named place, street and transit stop, with its category,
  address, hours, phone and website
- **Timetable**: the stops, lines, trips and walking transfers of every transit
  feed in the area, for two weeks
- **Streets**: a walking and cycling street graph, with one-way streets for
  bikes, stairs, bike lanes and busy roads

A background worker on the device loads them the first time you search or ask
for directions offline. Walking and cycling use a shortest-path search over the
street graph. Transit uses RAPTOR, a round-based search that finds the fastest
trip for each number of changes, with walks to and from stops along real
streets.
