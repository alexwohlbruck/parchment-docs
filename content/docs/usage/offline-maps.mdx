---
title: Offline maps
description: Save an area of the map, with the layers you choose, and see it with no connection.
---

Open **Offline maps** from the sidebar, or from the dashboard on a phone, then
choose **Save an area**.

1. Move and zoom the map until the area you want fills the square. The square
   stays still while the map moves, so zooming out saves a bigger area.
2. Pick what to include. The map itself is always saved. Layers that are on
   the map now start switched on: transit lines, cycling, street imagery,
   3D terrain and any custom layer with map tiles.
3. Check the size estimate under the switches, then tap **Download**.

The area is named after the place in its middle; rename it from its menu. A
square that would take too long to save turns red, and you need to zoom in
before you can download it.

## Managing saved areas

Each saved area shows its size and when it was last updated. From its menu you
can:

- **Update** it to pick up the latest map data
- **Rename** or **Delete** it

A download keeps going after a reload. If the connection drops it waits, and it
carries on once you're back online. Tap pause to stop it yourself.

Overlapping areas share what they have in common, so saving a neighbourhood
inside a city you already saved costs very little.

## What works offline

- The map and the layers you saved, inside the saved area
- Your location on the map
- Your saved places and collections

Search, directions and live transit times still need a connection. Offline
search and offline routing are planned.

## Keeping saved maps

Parchment asks the browser to keep saved maps when space runs low. On iPhone
and iPad, add Parchment to your home screen: Safari clears data for sites you
haven't opened in a while, but not for apps on the home screen.

Some layers can't be saved, because their providers don't allow it. These are
the OpenStreetMap standard tiles, Esri satellite imagery, OpenTopoMap,
OpenRailwayMap, OpenSeaMap and Mapbox layers. They are left out of the list.

## How it works

Saving an area lists every tile each chosen layer has inside the square, from
zoom 0 to the layer's deepest zoom (up to 16; 12 for terrain). It then saves the
tiles, plus the fonts, icons and manifests the map needs, to a Cache Storage
bucket called `offline-regions`. The service worker answers from that bucket
before anything else, so a saved tile is drawn with or without a connection.
Online, it refreshes the saved copy in the background.

The size estimate measures one real tile per layer at the centre of the square
and scales it for each zoom level.
