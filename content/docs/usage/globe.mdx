---
title: Globe view
description: Explore the globe with stars, sunlight, and city lights.
---

With the MapLibre map engine and Globe projection selected in Appearance, zoom
out to see Earth against a black sky with subtle stars and a blue atmosphere.
The sky follows the camera as you turn the globe, and the atmosphere follows its
edge when you tilt the map. A faint photographic Milky Way lies behind the
globe. A softly glowing sun appears at its real position
and disappears behind Earth when the globe blocks it.

The effect works with the standard, satellite, and hybrid basemaps, in either
app theme. It fades away as you zoom in so the usual map sky returns for closer
views.

![Globe with stars and a blue atmosphere](/images/globe-sky.png)

Turn on **Day & Night** in the layers selector to light the globe with the
current sun position. A soft twilight boundary separates day from night, the
atmosphere brightens on the sunlit side with pale daylight haze, and warm city lights appear after
sunset in satellite and hybrid views. The camera gradually increases exposure as the night side fills the view,
revealing more terrain and the Milky Way. It eases back down when you return to
daylight, while keeping the sun and city lights bright.

Satellite and hybrid globes show clouds above the surface, with warm twilight
and soft shadows. Their oceans have deeper blue tones and a soft sun reflection
that follows the camera and sun. The standard map keeps clouds and city lights
hidden.

Clouds use [Live Cloud Maps](https://github.com/matteason/live-cloud-maps),
containing modified EUMETSAT data. The provider publishes new images every three
hours; this is near-real-time satellite imagery, not a continuous live stream.
The 4K texture is about 1.5 MB and loads only in satellite/hybrid globe view.
Updates are checked every three hours while that view is visible. If the feed
is unavailable, the first load uses a bundled NASA Blue Marble composite;
later failures retain the last loaded image. Satellite data can have gaps or
delays, and polar cloud patterns are mirrored by the provider. Clouds fade away
with the atmosphere as you zoom in.

![Daylight globe with clouds](/images/globe-clouds.png)

![Satellite globe with sunlight, twilight, and city lights](/images/globe-sky-satellite.png)

City lights use [NASA's 2016 Black Marble composite](https://science.nasa.gov/earth/earth-observatory/earth-at-night/maps/),
not live observations. A compressed global texture loads only when Day & Night
is enabled in satellite or hybrid globe view; it needs no external imagery service or account.

![Nighttime Earth and the sun](/images/globe-sun.png)

The sky uses a [photographic Milky Way panorama by ESO/S. Brunier](https://www.eso.org/public/images/eso0932a/),
resized and compressed to about 1.5 MB. It downloads only in globe view and is
cached afterward. Its brightness stays subtle so Earth remains the focus.

![Night-side exposure and the Milky Way](/images/globe-milky-way.png)
