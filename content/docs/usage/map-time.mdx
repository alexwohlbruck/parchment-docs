title: Map time
description: See transit service and daylight at another time of the week.
---

Some layers change with the time: **Transit** draws only the lines running at
that moment, and **Day & Night** shades the parts of the Earth in darkness.
While either one is on, the layers selector shows a **Time** section under the
overlays.

- **Now** follows the clock and updates every minute. It is where every
  session starts; a chosen time is never restored after a reload.
- Tap a day or drag the slider to pick another time in the coming week. Every
  time-aware layer moves to it together, so the transit network and the
  terminator always show the same moment.
- Tap **Now** to go back to the live map.

Transit only reads the time while it shows **Live** service. Its **Full
service** look draws every line whenever it runs, so with only that on, the
time section is hidden.

With Day & Night on, the globe's sunlight, the Moon and the stars follow the
chosen time too.

## For developers

A temporal layer is a built-in layer group listed in `TEMPORAL_LAYERS`
(`web/src/lib/map-time/temporal-layers.ts`), optionally limited to the
appearances that read the time. The time control appears whenever
`activeTemporalLayerIds` returns at least one of them.

The chosen time lives in `useMapTimeStore` (`web/src/stores/map-time.store.ts`).
Layers read it one of two ways:

- From a store, by watching `useMapTimeStore().time`. The transit store does
  this and refilters the network.
- From a service or a custom layer, through `mapTime()` and
  `onMapTimeChange()` in `web/src/services/map/map-time.service.ts`. The
  Day & Night overlay redraws on change, and the globe sky reads `mapTime()`
  each frame. While live, `mapTime()` is the clock.

To add one: declare its group in `TEMPORAL_LAYERS`, then draw from `mapTime()`
or the store's `time` instead of `new Date()`.
