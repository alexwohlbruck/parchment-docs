      - content: >-
          Generate multiple trip options using different transportation modes
          and combinations. A request with more than two waypoints returns one
          whole journey per travel strategy, each carried from stop to stop — a
          journey that cycles to the first stop still has the bike at the
          second. Segments carry `legIndex` to say which hop they belong to. A
          transit leg's `routeOptions` lists the other lines running the same
          stops at that time. Its `shortName` names the line; a line with no
          GTFS short name reads by its long name, and the train number moves to
          `trip.shortName`. A walking segment with `streetTransfer` leaves the
          paid area between stations the agency does not connect, so the next
          ride is a new fare. Where the station graph knows the station, a
          transit leg's `transitDetails` carry `transfer` (how the change from
          the previous ride is made: its class, time and the walk), `entrance`
          and `exit` (the entrance used for the first ride and the exit after
          the last, with the walk inside the station) and `boardingPosition`
          (where to stand on the train). A station walk that is not an
          `estimate` carries `geometry`, a level per vertex and written `steps`.
          Street instructions carry `exitNumber` for roundabouts and, where the
          map signposts the maneuver, `exitRef` (a motorway exit's number) and
          `destination` (where the signs point). Street segments carry
          `hazards`: crosswalks, traffic lights, stop and give-way signs, rail
          and tram crossings, tunnels, fords, steep grades, stairs, sharp
          curves, low clearances, school zones, where a bike lane ends or starts
          partway along a road, and the like, placed by distance along the
          segment. Some depend on conditions: unlit paths after sunset,
          flood-prone roads when rain is forecast, bridges at 2 °C or below, and
          speed cameras only where warning of them is lawful. Comfort hazards
          (rough surfaces, dismount sections, ferries, tolls, traffic calming,
          movable bridges, speed cameras, bike lanes starting) appear only for
          users with premium navigation. Driving and cycling segments carry
          `drivingSide`, and where the routing engine knows the lanes,
          `laneGeometry`: the route drawn in the lanes it takes (for cycling,
          the bike lane, or beside the traffic), with `measures` giving the
          distance along `geometry` each coordinate stands for. Driving segments
          also carry `lanePlan`: per instruction index, the approach lanes left
          to right and which lead on. An origin with `heading` starts driving
          routes, and cycling routes above 4 m/s `speed`, in that direction;
          walking routes ignore it. A trip with transit legs may carry
          `departureProfile`: for each catchable run of its first transit leg,
          when to leave, when it arrives through the connections on the stop
          boards, and the tightest transfer. Runs stop where the boards end;
          `validUntil` is the last leave time covered.

      - content: >-
          Rough door-to-door seconds for each requested mode between the
          waypoints, in order, without planning full trips: no geometry,
          alternatives or vehicle pickups. A mode comes back `null` when it has
          no route, or is out of reach (a walk over 100 km, a ride over 300 km).
          Transit is the earliest-arriving ride departing at `departureTime`,
          and is only estimated between two waypoints. Results are cached
          briefly. Requires premium navigation.
        heading: estimate-travel-time-per-mode
