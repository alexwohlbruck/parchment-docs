---
title: Indoor maps
description: How Barrelman builds floor plans from OpenStreetMap indoor tagging and serves them as the indoor tile bundle.
---

Barrelman turns OpenStreetMap's [Simple Indoor Tagging](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Simple_Indoor_Tagging) into floor plans and serves them as vector tiles. Parchment draws them with a floor picker; see [Indoor maps](/usage/indoor-maps) for what users see.

Everything is built in one materialized view, `indoor_features` (`import/create-indoor-views.sql`), with one row per feature per level it stands on. A client shows a floor with a single `level == n` filter.

---

## Tiles

Two Martin sources read views over `indoor_features`, and the `indoor` bundle (`src/config/tile-bundles.ts`) serves both in one tile:

```
GET /tiles/indoor/{z}/{x}/{y}
```

### `indoor_venues` (zoom 15–17)

One outline per venue: a terminal, a mall, a station's concourses. It arrives a zoom before the plans, so a client knows a floor plan is there before it draws one.

| Property | Description |
|---|---|
| `venue` | Venue id |
| `name` | The airport's, when the venue spans several named terminals; else its biggest named building's, a car park only failing all others; else the nearest station's |
| `levels` | Its levels, `;`-separated: `-1;0;1` |
| `level_names` | Signage names by level: `-1=B1;0=G` |
| `default_level` | The level it opens on |
| `buildings` | `;`-separated ids of the building outlines it stands in, for a client to hide while the plan shows |

### `indoor_map` (zoom 16–17)

The plans themselves. Every row carries `venue` and `default_level`, so a style can show one venue at a chosen level and every other at its own default.

| Property | Description |
|---|---|
| `venue`, `level`, `default_level` | The venue, the level this row is drawn on, and the venue's default |
| `kind` | `floor`, `wall`, `area`, `line`, `flight` or `poi` |
| `class` | What the feature is (below) |
| `subclass` | Detail for styling: a room's use, an escalator's `conveying`, a door's `entrance` value, a POI's tag value, `security` for a screening checkpoint, or `jet_bridge` |
| `name`, `ref` | From the OSM feature; `ref` carries a gate's number |
| `id` | The OSM feature's place id |
| `width`, `length`, `bearing` | On `flight` rows: the flight's width and length in metres, and the bearing it climbs, in degrees |

| Kind | Rows | Classes |
|---|---|---|
| `floor` | Each level's walkable outline. Above ground it also covers the venue's buildings, so a sparsely mapped floor still reads as the building | — |
| `wall` | Room outlines (not stairwells or lift shafts), mapped walls and the floor's edge, merged per level and buffered to a drawn thickness (60 cm inside, 1 m outside), with openings cut at doors | — |
| `area` | Rooms, areas, corridors, platforms, columns, stairs, lift shafts and jet bridges | `room`, `area`, `corridor`, `level`, `column`, `platform`, `stairs`, `elevator`, `wall` |
| `line` | Steps, escalators, indoor footways and platforms mapped as lines. A flight has none on the level it climbs from | `steps`, `escalator`, `corridor`, `platform` |
| `flight` | A point at the middle of each stair or escalator that climbs between levels, on the lower level | `steps`, `escalator` |
| `poi` | Shops, amenities, gates, doors, lifts, a label point for each named room, and a point at the middle of each flight of steps for its icon | The POI's key (`shop`, `amenity`, `aeroway`, `railway`, …), `door`, `elevator`, `steps`, `escalator`, `room` |

Door openings use the door's `width`, defaulting to 0.9 m and clamped between 0.7 m and 4 m.

**Flights.** A stair or escalator line on more than one level, with `incline=up` or `incline=down`, becomes a flight on its lower level, climbing in the direction `incline` gives. Its `length` runs from end to end of the line, and it is 1.5 m wide (1.2 m for an escalator) unless `width` says otherwise. A `down` flight within 6 m of a parallel `up` one climbs the same way, since `down` is often tagged for where an escalator carries you rather than how its way slopes. Clients draw the flight as a model, rising a storey over its length.

**Jet bridges.** An `aeroway=jet_bridge` line within about 50 m of a venue joins the nearest one as a corridor (`class=corridor`, `subclass=jet_bridge`), 3 m wide unless `width` says otherwise (clamped to 2–6 m). It stands on its `level`, or on the venue's default level, and is part of that level's floor and walls.

## Which OSM features are read

A feature is considered when it has `level`, `repeat_on` or `indoor`, is a platform (`railway=platform` or `public_transport=platform`), or is an `aeroway=gate`. Anything tagged `indoor=no`, and building outlines without `indoor`, are left out.

| Tags | Becomes |
|---|---|
| `indoor=room` / `area` / `corridor` / `level` / `column` | An area of that class. `room=stairs` or `stairs=yes` is stairs; `highway=elevator` or `room=elevator` is a lift shaft |
| `indoor=wall` (area or line) | A wall |
| `highway=steps` | Steps, or an escalator with a `conveying` value other than `no` |
| `highway=footway` / `path` / `pedestrian` with `indoor`, or `highway=corridor` | An indoor footway |
| Platforms | Only indoors or underground: with `indoor`, a negative `level` or `layer`, `location=underground` or `tunnel`. An open-air platform is the basemap's |
| `shop`, `amenity`, `tourism`, `office`, `healthcare`, `craft`, `leisure`, `aeroway=gate`, station entrances, ticket validators | A POI. A gate also tagged as a door counts as a gate |
| `aeroway=jet_bridge` (line) | A jet bridge, with or without indoor tags |
| `door`, `entrance`, `indoor=door` | A door, and an opening in the wall it sits on |
| `amenity=security_control`, `police=checkpoint`, `room=security_check`, `aeroway=security_checkpoint` | Drawn as one screening zone (`subclass=security`) |
| `level:ref` | The level's name on the signs |

### Levels

A feature's levels are its `level` plus any `repeat_on`. Lists (`0;1`), ranges (`0-2`, `-4--2`), decimals (`-0.5`) and any order are understood. Unparseable parts and anything past 200 floors either way are skipped rather than failing the feature, and a feature is drawn on at most 64 levels.

Where `level` is missing:

- A platform or indoor feature with a `layer` from `-1` to `-9` takes that as its level, one storey per layer.
- Otherwise an indoor area, corridor, wall, column or level outline goes on level 0.
- A gate goes on its venue's default level.
- Anything else is left out, even with `repeat_on`.

## How venues are derived

**Clustering.** Indoor shapes (not points) within about 25 m of each other form one venue. Clustering runs per cell of a quarter-degree grid, so a venue straddling a grid line splits in two. A cluster becomes a venue only with at least three rooms, areas, corridors, platforms or level outlines: a lone indoor passage is not a building to step into. The venue's outline is the convex hull of its shapes.

**Points.** A shop, door or other point joins the venue whose outline contains it. Inside two, it joins the smaller, so it is drawn once.

**Levels.** A venue's levels are those its rooms, areas, corridors, platforms and level outlines stand on. Each is named by the commonest `level:ref` (or `indoor=level` name) among the single-level shapes on it, up to 12 characters.

**Default level.** For an airport, the level most of its gates are on. Otherwise level 0 if the venue has one, else its busiest level, so a station that is entirely below ground opens on its concourse.

**Buildings.** The above-ground building and building-part outlines the venue stands in, for a client to clear while the plan shows:

- outlines whose footprint is more than 30% covered by the venue's rooms at ground level or above;
- parts, roofs and annexes at least 40% inside those or the rooms;
- `building=roof` canopies touching them.

The venue's own outline is not used here: under a station it can run beneath whole blocks of towers. Underground outlines (`location=underground`) are never cleared.

## Building and refreshing

The view stores its rows rather than computing them per tile, because floors and walls union whole levels.

- **Creation.** The API creates the view (empty) at startup, and fills it in the background if it has never been populated. `scripts/import-osm.sh` creates and fills it after an import.
- **Refresh.** `scripts/update-osm.sh` refreshes it after every OSM update. To refresh by hand, run the **Refresh Indoor Maps** task in the admin console, or:

  ```bash
  scripts/refresh-view.sh indoor_features
  ```

  A populated view refreshes concurrently, so tiles keep serving the old rows until the new ones are ready.
- **Index.** `import/create-detail-indexes.sql` builds the partial indexes `geo_places_indoor_geom_idx` and `geo_places_jet_bridge_geom_idx` concurrently. Without the first, the refresh scans all of `geo_places`; on a New York import it took 169 s without the index and about 4 s with it.
- **Schema changes.** The view carries a version comment (`v10`). A changed query needs it bumped: the next run then drops and recreates the view.

<Callout type="warn">
A deployment with its own `martin-config.yaml` needs the `indoor_venues`, `indoor_map` and `fixtures` entries added, including `height` on `indoor_map`. Start the API first so the views exist: Martin exits on a source whose table is missing.
</Callout>

## Fixtures

Outside floor plans, the `fixtures` view (`import/create-detail-views.sql`) holds what a person finds their way by at street level. It is served as the `fixtures` source in the `detail` bundle, zoom 16–17, with these properties:

| Property | Description |
|---|---|
| `kind` | `station_entrance` (`railway=subway_entrance` or `train_station_entrance`), `elevator` (`highway=elevator`) or `entrance` (`entrance=main` or `yes`) |
| `ref` | The entrance's `ref` |
| `id` | The OSM feature's place id |

Only points are read, and anything indoor or underground (`indoor`, `location=indoor` or `underground`) or private (`access=private` or `no`) is left out: indoor ones belong to floor plans. The view is refreshed with `indoor_features`: after imports and OSM updates, by the **Refresh Fixtures** console task, or with `scripts/refresh-view.sh fixtures`.

## How Parchment draws it

Parchment's MapLibre style adds the `indoor` source and drives it through style state keys, so changing floors rebuilds no filters:

| State | Holds |
|---|---|
| `indoorVenue` | The venue the floor picker serves, `0` for none |
| `indoorLevel` | The level shown in that venue; `1000` shows its outside |
| `indoorBuildings` | `;id;` list of building ids hidden from the 3D buildings layer |
| `indoorPlaces` | Basemap ids of the shops and gates a showing floor plan labels itself, hidden from the basemap's labels |
| `indoorFixtures` | Ids of the doors, lifts and station entrances a showing floor plan signs, hidden from the fixtures layer |

Every other venue shows its `default_level`, or its outside when that level is below ground. A venue counts as showing when it is on a floor rather than its outside. Fixtures are drawn by `web/src/lib/map-style/fixture-layers.ts`: station entrances and lifts from zoom 17, building entrances from zoom 18. The indoor layers live in `web/src/lib/map-style/indoor-layers.ts`, the picker logic in `web/src/lib/indoor/indoor-venue.ts` and `web/src/services/map/indoor.service.ts`.
